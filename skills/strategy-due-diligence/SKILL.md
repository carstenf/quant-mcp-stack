# Strategy Due Diligence (core: intake -> provenance -> adapter -> verdict -> tradability audit -> report)

Use when someone hands over a third-party strategy (newsletter, author, fund, colleague; often traded by hand, not generated by a backtester) and wants to know whether it is real, robust and investable. **Default stance: you do not know whether the history was filtered** (cherry-picked periods, removed weeks, edited lists, back-filled before publication, best of many variants). The skill must find out what can be found out and price in what cannot. Output: short chat summary + PDF report in the user's language (default: multi-page with a stand-alone executive summary on page 1; one-page C-level version only on request).

## Structure: core + adapter
This file is the asset-class-neutral core. Pick the adapter from what was handed over and read it before step 1:
- **Equities** (pick lists, stock trade lists): [references/equities.md](references/equities.md) - data, ML4T backtest, ml4t-diagnostic, TradeBlocks robustness, own-code gaps, and the script parameters the core refers to.
- **Options** (Option Omega parameters or shared backtest, trade logs, alerts, broker statements): [references/options.md](references/options.md).
Section numbers 1 and 3-6 live in the adapter; 0, 2 and 7-10 are here. Where the core names a statistic, the adapter says which tool produces it.

## House rules first
If the user has their own conventions (a workflow/entry skill, project folder for research files, a holdout rule and protocol, a chart style file, a factor library), they override the defaults below. Look for them before starting (user's skills, memory, project files) and follow them; name every deviation from this skill in the report.
- Holdout: due diligence fits nothing, so it needs no locked holdout of its own. The vendor's overfitting is tested by the period after the first verifiable publication (the vendor's holdout); our own choices are covered by counting variants (PSR/DSR). If the user keeps a holdout rule, still log every run in their protocol (what was seen contaminates their own research on that period, and the results must not feed the selection of their own factors or strategies). The moment a third-party strategy is rebuilt as an own one (section 8, path 1), the user's holdout rule applies in full.
- Storage: default is the ML4T workspace; use the user's project folder if they have one (`run_python` / `job_start` accept `cwd`).

## Golden rule: use the tested tools, do not reinvent them
- Each adapter names its tested tools (backtester, statistics library, TradeBlocks). Never hand-roll what they offer; own code only for real gaps, and say so in the report.
- Methodology reference: Jansen, *Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading* 3e (survivorship and point-in-time universes ch. 2, cost sensitivity and baselines ch. 16, costs and capacity ch. 18). If the user has Jansen's ML4T agent skills installed (e.g. `ml4t-survivorship-bias`, `ml4t-transaction-costs`, `ml4t-backtest-overfitting`, `ml4t-deflated-sharpe`), consult them and name deviations.

## 0. Intake (ask only if unknown)
- Rules as claimed: when the list is known (must be before the entry open -> no look-ahead), entry/exit timing (e.g. Monday open to next Monday open), weighting (equal), number of names, holding period, filters.
- **Track-record type** (always ask; it sets the evidence level) - who produced the record and how:
  - `audited`: real money with verifiable statements (broker statements, third-party verified performance, GIPS composite) - real fills and costs;
  - `self-reported`: real money, but only the vendor's own figures - counts as `paper-vendor` until statements are seen;
  - `paper-independent`: signals recorded by an independent party (the user, a colleague) as they arrived, ideally with timestamps (mails, chat, a ledger) - the vendor could not omit or back-date anything; fills simulated (often open/close without costs);
  - `paper-vendor`: paper or model portfolio published by the vendor with timestamps - out-of-sample only from the first verifiable publication, but the vendor chose what to publish;
  - `unknown`: unknown or hypothetical (e.g. produced by a backtest, reconstructed) - in-sample only.
  Also ask: who recorded it, since when, is every signal included (no gaps), are there timestamps? Independently recorded lists can be added to a timestamp ledger afterwards with their original timestamps as evidence.
- **Provenance** (the key questions for a third-party list): Since when is each list verifiably public with a timestamp (emails, posts, newsletter archive, archive.org snapshot)? Was any part delivered or reconstructed later? Did the rules ever change? Does the vendor publish other strategies/lists (only the survivors shown?). Does the vendor publish an own equity curve or real fills?
- **Traded rule:** which holding period / rule was actually traded or recorded before anyone looked at the results? -> `--traded-hold` (no DSR over holds, PSR instead).
- **Vendor variants:** how many lists/strategies/models does the vendor run or has run? This sets `--assumed-vendor-trials` (unknown -> 100).

- Asset-class specifics (benchmarks, costs, script or tool parameters): see the adapter.

**Evidence grade** (`_evidence_grade`, page 1 of the report): **A** = a record nobody could filter (`audited`, or `paper-independent` that is complete and timestamped), out-of-sample period >= MinTRL, paired benchmark test p < 0,05 and still significant at the assumed vendor trials - without real fills on the conservative cost level; **B** = genuine out-of-sample evidence that does not meet all of this (too short, not significant, or recorded by the vendor); **C** = no verifiable out-of-sample period (`unknown` or no start date). Only grade A can support "Buy" on the record alone. A grade B because of length is not a provenance problem: say so and give the remaining time to MinTRL.

## 2. Provenance and filter check (third-party lists)
The history is in-sample until proven otherwise. Only the period after the first verifiable publication is genuine out-of-sample.
1. **Data checks** (`data_checks.json` from the script): missing weeks (a skipped week; holiday weeks starting on Tuesday are not flagged), duplicate rows, list size changing, identical consecutive lists, tickers without prices, names picked before their first price or after their last price, unusual weekday of the week start, list turnover per week. Missing weeks can be removed bad weeks; report each finding.
2. **Publication split** (`--published-from`): metrics before vs. after the first verifiable publication (`_publication_split`; the post part has bootstrap CIs and the paired benchmark test). A strong drop after publication is the classic sign of a fitted or filtered record (cf. McLean & Pontiff 2016 for published anomalies).
3. **Unknown number of vendor trials** (`_vendor_trials_sensitivity`): PSR of the leader's excess return against the expected maximum Sharpe of N zero-skill strategies (`compute_expected_max_sharpe`) for N = 1, 10, 100, 1000, plus the N at which it stops being significant. A record that survives only N <= 10 is fragile if the vendor runs many lists.
4. **Entry-delay test** (`_entry_delay`): entry 1, 2, 5 trading days later. Genuine weekly signals decay smoothly; an edge that collapses with a one-day delay, or picks whose run-up happened before the pick week, point to late or back-dated lists.
5. **Vendor curve vs. replication:** if the vendor publishes returns or fills, import them into TradeBlocks next to the replicated tape and run `compare_backtest_to_actual` / `analyze_discrepancies`. Systematic gaps (vendor better in exactly the bad weeks) indicate filtering.
6. **Not testable here** - list explicitly: survivorship of the vendor's own strategies (only winners shown), undisclosed rule changes, selection of the start date. Treat these as risks with a haircut, not as passed.
7. **Grids are leads, not findings (plateau rule).** Whenever a setting is picked from a grid - holding periods, entry times, deltas, filter thresholds, optimizer cells, whether by the vendor or by us - apply the plateau rule of the TradeBlocks skills (`is-this-optimum-real`; `optimize/references/plateau-and-verdict.md` where installed): state metric, neighbourhood, tolerance and tie breaker before choosing; take the central actual cell of the broadest stable region; flag boundary winners, cliffs, flat grids and one- or two-cell regions; recheck with a looser tolerance. If the leader and the centre differ, test the leader against the centre with a paired test on jointly held days (TradeBlocks `paired_bootstrap_comparison`, both arms in one block with distinct strategy labels). A vendor rule that sits on a spike is a filter warning even if its own record looks good.
8. **Minimum improvement, stated before looking.** Before any comparison (strategy vs. benchmark, leader vs. centre, filtered vs. unfiltered) write down the smallest difference that would matter for trading (e.g. 0,1 Sharpe, 2 pp CAGR after costs). A significant difference below it counts as "no improvement".
9. **Dependence on few days or names.** Report the share of total P&L from the best 1, 5 and 10 days (and, for pick lists, the top contributors from the adapter), the result without them, and per-year results. An edge that disappears without one day or one name is a single-episode record.

## 7. Interpretation rules
- p-values primary, as decimals (p = 0,07), t-value in brackets; thresholds p < 0,05 (t >= 2), quant hurdle p < 0,003 (t >= 3); DSR > 0,95; PBO < 0,20.
- Results depend on sampling frequency (daily vs weekly) - state which one each number uses; prefer the engine's daily marked curve for risk, weekly non-overlapping returns for inference on weekly strategies.
- "Beats the benchmark" needs the paired test: `vs_benchmark` p < 0,05 and the CI of the Sharpe difference / information ratio above 0. A high absolute Sharpe with a CI that includes the benchmark is not outperformance.
- Report CIs next to point estimates (e.g. Sharpe 1,98 [0,56; 3,42]).
- **Out-of-sample first:** the verdict rests on the verified post-publication period; the pre-publication history counts only as supporting evidence. If there is no verifiable publication date, the whole record is in-sample.
- Investable only if: verified out-of-sample period >= MinTRL and significant on its own (paired benchmark test p < 0,05), random-test p < 0,05 in both halves and post-publication, factor-model alpha p < 0,05 (ideally < 0,003), DSR > 0,95 (PSR for a pre-specified traded rule) and still significant at a realistic number of vendor trials, no significant edge decay or post-publication break, entry-delay decay smooth, costs/capacity acceptable at the conservative cost level, no unexplained data-check findings. Otherwise: "Watch / small satellite": record the vendor's lists live from now on with timestamps (the only hard proof), re-test in 12 months, with explicit scale-up and stop criteria.
- Always state what is not tested (vendor survivorship, real fills, crisis periods, global factors, taxes, FX).
- **Verdict language:** report every test with its result, the population it used and its limit; say whether it accounts for selection from a grid; diagnostics that reuse the selection history (walk-forward, Monte Carlo, edge decay, per-year) are single-tape diagnostics, not out-of-sample confirmation; never call anything robust from one rank, one interval or one test; an interval crossing zero does not prove equivalence; end with the missing evidence and the next discriminating test.

## 8. Tradability audit (for grade B/C - make an unknown strategy tradable or reject it)
The record itself cannot become reliable; reliability has to come from data the vendor could not influence. Pick the path that fits and say which one in the report.
1. **Rules can be reconstructed** (the list follows recognisable criteria, e.g. momentum + valuation): rebuild the signal generator on point-in-time data from the factors/rules you can identify, measure the overlap with the vendor's lists (share of names matched per week, rank correlation), and treat it from then on as an own strategy: research only on data the user allows for research, then a single pre-registered test on untouched data (if the user keeps a locked holdout, this needs their explicit permission for the run - the exemption for re-checking third-party lists no longer applies once it becomes an own strategy). Count every rebuild variant for the DSR.
2. **Black box** (lists only, no rules): incubate forward. From today every list is stored on receipt in an append-only ledger with UTC timestamp and SHA-256, ideally committed to a git remote for an independent timestamp (and flagged if it arrived after the open it is meant for). Pre-register before the first list: minimum duration (MinTRL from the backtest's Sharpe, at least 6 months), the test (paired excess return vs. benchmark on weekly non-overlapping returns, checked monthly with a sequential bound so repeated looks do not inflate errors), stop rule (e.g. drawdown beyond the backtest's 95 % Monte-Carlo quantile or excess-return t < -1 after 13 weeks), and stages: paper -> small real money (e.g. 10 % of target size) -> full size, each only after its hurdle.
3. **Returns explained by known factors** (factor alpha not significant, high R²): no need to trust the vendor - trade the factor exposure directly (factor ETF or own factor portfolio), cheaper and transparent.
Outcome, with explicit criteria in the report: **tradable** (grade A, or path 1 passed its untouched-data test, or path 2 passed its pre-registered hurdle), **incubating** (path 2 running; state the next decision date and hurdle), **reject** (stop rule hit, path 1 failed, or nothing beyond factor exposure - then recommend path 3).

## 9. Report (HTML -> PDF, A4, page footer with page numbers and "not investment advice")

Number every figure and table ("Abbildung 1", "Tabelle 1" with a caption, in the report language) and refer to them by number in the text. Heatmaps print the value in each cell (large enough to read; split into several figures if needed).
Directly under the report title, as the next line: the source of the strategy - for a video `channel · "video title" · publication date`, otherwise `author/vendor · "title" · date` - so the reader sees what was tested before the verdict. Page 1 executive summary: verdict badge (Buy / Conditional / Watch / Reject), evidence grade (A/B/C) and tradability outcome (tradable / incubating / reject, with path), 3-sentence key box, 6 KPI tiles (CAGR, CAGR after costs, Sharpe [CI], win rate with payoff ratio (% of 3:1 only for Minervini-style trade strategies), random-test p, factor-alpha p), checklist table (criterion, result, hurdle, met/borderline/not met; include provenance: track-record type and who recorded it, verified OOS length, publication break, vendor-trials break-even), next steps.
Sections: 1 strategy, provenance & data (data-check findings, sources per ticker, delistings); 2 performance (equity + drawdown charts, KPI table, in-sample vs. verified out-of-sample); 3 skill or luck (p-value reader box, random-portfolio histogram, baseline comparison, cumulative excess, regression scatter, DSR / vendor-trials / MinTRL / White's RC table); 4 where returns come from (factor loadings chart with CI, return attribution waterfall, daily vs weekly table); 5 concentration & robustness (contributors, HHI, sectors, trim/ex-theme table); 6 implementation (holding-period table, cost grid and break-evens, safety margin, entry delay, exit rules, rank analysis, liquidity and capacity); 7 edge decay & Monte Carlo (TradeBlocks); 8 risks (severity, likelihood, mitigant; untestable filter risks) + bull/bear case; 9 recommendation, tradability audit path & decision criteria (incl. incubation plan if applicable); appendix methodology, glossary, sources.
Charts: the user's chart style file if they have one; otherwise white background, Inter/Arial, no frame, light y-grid, y-label with unit, series names in line colour under the title (no legend box), solid equal-width lines, max 5 series, palette #2a78d6 #eb6834 #1baf7a #eda100 #c2407a #6250d6, blue = positive / red = negative only for signed values, take-away caption under each chart. **Background follows the medium:** the PDF always uses light (white-background) charts; dark charts only for chat/phone. If the style file has modes, render both (`chart_x.png` dark, `chart_x_light.png` light) from one script kept in the report folder. Check every PNG before building the PDF.
Report text rules: state the execution timing in plain words in section 1 and the appendix (entry day and price, exit day and price, whether weekends are held) and verify it against the trade log fill prices; every regression/factor table shows the coefficient AND its p-value for every factor, not only the significant ones, and names each factor in words (e.g. Investment = CMA, conservative minus aggressive investing firms); say why each multiple-testing statistic is there (DSR: several variants were tried and the best was taken); keep the body tables compact and readable (abbreviations such as KI/CI, PSR, MinTRL, IR are fine there, with at most a one-line pointer like 'KI = 95 % confidence interval, explained in Appendix B'); put all explanations into **Appendix B - glossary for readers without statistics or finance background**: every term AND every abbreviation used in the report (statistics, CAGR, p. a., Pp, TR, benchmark/index tickers, data-source tables, factor codes, quarter labels, OTC/ADR, tool names, and the company tickers mentioned), written out, with what it means and, for the key statistics (confidence interval, p-value, PSR/DSR, MinTRL, random portfolios), a worked example using this report's own numbers; before building the PDF, scan the body for capitalised abbreviations and check each one is in Appendix B; when numbers change versus an earlier report, give the main reason with its size and say whether the data source changed.

## 10. Deliver
Send the PDF and key charts (if the report lives on a remote server and the chat can only attach local files, move the file without passing its bytes through the model context - see the user's house rules for how); short reply with a table of the main numbers (incl. win rate and payoff ratio; % of the 3:1 target only for Minervini-style trade strategies); explicitly correct earlier numbers if a later step changes them.
